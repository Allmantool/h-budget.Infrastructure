## Common-cutoff rule

The durable acceptance boundary for an Accounting command is its SQL acceptance/outbox transaction, not Kafka publication, EventStore append time, payment date, or a user-supplied timestamp. At cutoff `C`, every command accepted at or before `C` must be present in an authoritative captured store or in captured pending work that recovery can replay exactly once. Mongo reference mutations and Rates SQL mutations need their own commit frontiers; EventStore replay cannot reconstruct their historical state.

The protected cutoff advances only after all component captures are complete, checksums and chain dependencies validate, ciphertext is stored outside the source-host/disk failure domain, and the final manifest is atomically published. Creation of a local file, an incomplete copy, or a failed verification does not advance it.

## Reset boundary summary

- Projection rebuild changes only derived projection histories, balances, heads, and checkpoints.
- Ledger reset prepares an inactive new generation and isolates selected-ledger business and asynchronous state; it preserves control/audit, shared Rates by default, other ledgers, identity, deployment configuration, and retained backups.
- Environment-storage reset may physically recreate only positively identified dedicated Home Ledger resources under a separately confirmed administrator plan. Names, URLs, or Docker aliases are insufficient identity evidence.

## Evidence limitations

This is a source/configuration audit, not production verification. Actual database builds/editions, recovery models, data sizes, change rates, mounted storage identities, backup destinations, encryption keys, notification routes, and recovery-host capacity were not inspected because the live host is explicitly out of scope. Their absence is a capability blocker and must not be replaced by assumed values.
